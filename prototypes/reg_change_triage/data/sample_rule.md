# Illustrative Guidance on Third-Party AI Systems in Financial Institutions

### § 1 Purpose

This guidance describes supervisory expectations for financial institutions
that use artificial intelligence systems obtained from third parties. It
applies to models used in credit, fraud, marketing, servicing and operations.

### § 2 Inventory

An institution must maintain a complete inventory of all third-party AI
models in use, including the vendor, the business use case, the model owner
and the assigned risk tier. The inventory must be updated within 30 days of
any new deployment or retirement.

### § 3 Risk tiering

An institution must assign a risk tier to each third-party AI model before
it is deployed. The tier must reflect customer impact, financial materiality
and the degree of automation in the decision.

### § 4 Vendor due diligence

Before entering a contract, an institution must perform due diligence on the
AI vendor. Due diligence must cover the vendor's data sources, training
practices, information security program and financial condition.

### § 5 Independent validation

Models assigned to the highest risk tier must be independently validated
before first use and at least annually thereafter. Validation must be
performed by staff who did not develop or select the model.

### § 6 Ongoing monitoring

An institution must monitor the performance of each third-party AI model on
an ongoing basis, including accuracy, drift and outcome stability. High-tier
models must be reviewed at least monthly.

### § 7 Fair lending testing

AI models used in credit decisions must be tested for disparate impact on
protected classes before deployment and at least quarterly while in use.

### § 8 Adverse action explanations

When an AI model contributes to an adverse credit decision, the institution
must be able to provide the applicant with the specific principal reasons
for the decision.

### § 9 Human oversight

An institution must define when a qualified person reviews an AI-driven
decision before it takes effect, and must ensure staff can override the
model's output. Thresholds for human review must be documented and approved.

### § 10 Customer data sharing

An institution must not share customer personal information with an AI
vendor unless the contract restricts the vendor's use, retention and onward
transfer of that information.

### § 11 Incident reporting

Material incidents involving a third-party AI model must be escalated to
senior management within 72 hours of discovery and reported to the board at
least quarterly.

### § 12 Generative AI output controls

Where generative AI produces content for customers, an institution must
implement controls to detect and prevent inaccurate, fabricated or
misleading statements before the content is delivered.

### § 13 Records

An institution must retain documentation of model selection, validation,
monitoring results and material decisions for at least five years.

### § 14 Board oversight

The board of directors must approve the institution's risk appetite for the
use of AI at least annually.

### § 15 Exit planning

For each critical AI vendor, an institution must maintain a documented exit
plan that allows the service to be replaced or brought in-house without
material disruption.

### § 16 Effective date

This guidance takes effect 180 days after publication. Supervisors will
consider an institution's progress in examinations that begin after that
date.

### § 17 Staff training

Staff who use AI tools in their work must receive training on the tools'
limitations and on how to escalate concerns about model outputs.
